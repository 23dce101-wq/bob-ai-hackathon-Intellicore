# Problem Statement — PortPredict AI

## The Problem

The global maritime logistics industry faces a critical bottleneck at port terminals. The 2021 LA/Long Beach port crisis saw 100+ vessels anchored offshore for weeks, creating a supply chain domino effect that cost the global economy over $10B in delayed goods, increased freight costs, and production shutdowns.

## Who Is Affected

- **Port Operators:** Must manually allocate berths, cranes, and yard space across hundreds of vessels using spreadsheets and radio communications. Decisions are reactive, made after congestion has already built up.
- **Shift Supervisors:** Receive handover notes with incomplete information, lacking a unified view of upcoming 72-hour operational pressure.
- **Vessel Operators:** Face unpredictable waiting times, escalating demurrage fees ($30,000-$80,000/day for large container ships), and fuel waste at anchorage.
- **Supply Chain Downstream:** Every day of port delay cascades into weeks of retail stockouts, manufacturing pauses, and Just-In-Time system failures.

## Why Existing Solutions Fall Short

| Existing Approach | Limitation |
|---|---|
| Manual spreadsheet planning | Cannot process hundreds of overlapping scheduling constraints in real time |
| Rule-based TOS systems | Static rules fail when multiple disruptions occur simultaneously (weather + crane failure + surge arrivals) |
| Reactive congestion alerts | By the time congestion is detected, vessels are already queued and demurrage is accruing |
| Generic AI chatbots | Produce plausible but ungrounded recommendations without access to the actual schedule state |

## Why This Problem Matters Now

Global vessel traffic has grown 40% since 2019. Climate change is increasing the frequency of port-disrupting weather events. The industry is operating at the edge of capacity with 20th-century planning tools. A proactive, explainable, data-driven approach is not a luxury — it is an operational necessity.
